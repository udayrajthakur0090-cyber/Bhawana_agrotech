THE FUTURE OF FARMING

What is Hydroponics?

Hydroponics is a method of growing plants without traditional soil. Instead, plants receive essential nutrients through a water-based nutrient solution. In some systems, roots are supported by inert growing media such as cocopeat, perlite or rockwool.

The system provides plants with water, oxygen and nutrients according to their growing requirements. Different hydroponic techniques can be selected depending on crop type, available space and production goals.

OUR GROWING SYSTEMS

Types of Hydroponics Systems

Explore different hydroponic technologies and find a system suited to your crop and farm requirements.

01 / HYDROPONIC SYSTEM

Deep Water Culture Systems

Deep Water Culture (DWC) is a hydroponic method in which plants are held in net pots above a reservoir containing nutrient-rich, oxygenated water. Plant roots remain submerged in the solution, receiving continuous access to water and nutrients.

Key Benefits

  • Continuous access to water and nutrients
  • Simple system layout and maintenance
  • Suitable for many leafy green crops
02 / HYDROPONIC SYSTEM

Nutrient Film Technique (NFT)

NFT systems grow plants in channels through which a thin, continuous film of nutrient solution flows over the roots. The channels are installed at a gentle slope, allowing the solution to return to a reservoir for recirculation.

Key Benefits

  • Efficient circulation of nutrient solution
  • Modular design for scalable installations
  • Root exposure to air and flowing nutrients
03 / HYDROPONIC SYSTEM

Dutch Bucket Grow System

The Dutch Bucket, also known as the Bato Bucket system, uses individual growing containers connected to shared irrigation and drainage lines. It is commonly used for larger crops that need more root space and growing media.

Key Benefits

  • Expandable arrangement of connected buckets
  • Controlled irrigation and nutrient delivery
  • Suitable for tomatoes, peppers and cucumbers
04 / HYDROPONIC SYSTEM

Aeroponic Systems

Aeroponics is a soilless growing technique in which plant roots are suspended in air and supplied with a fine mist of nutrient solution. The system uses misting equipment and a controlled delivery arrangement to nourish exposed roots.

Key Benefits

  • Efficient delivery of nutrient mist to roots
  • Reduced growing footprint with vertical designs
  • High root exposure to oxygen

BENEFITS OF SOILLESS FARMING

Advantages of Hydroponics

Hydroponic cultivation offers several advantages when the system is properly designed and managed.

01

Year-Round Cultivation

Plan crop cycles across different seasons with suitable environmental and nutrient management.

02

Efficient Water Use

Recirculating systems can reuse nutrient solution and help reduce water wastage.

03

Space Optimization

Suitable layouts, channels and vertical structures can increase use of available space.

04

Managed Nutrition

Growers can monitor and adjust nutrient concentration according to crop requirements.

05

Flexible Locations

Hydroponic systems can be planned for greenhouses, rooftops and selected indoor spaces.

06

Cleaner Growing Setup

Soilless cultivation can reduce exposure to some soil-related weeds and pests.

07

Convenient Harvesting

Raised channels, benches and containers can make crop access and harvesting easier.

08

Modular Expansion

Many hydroponic systems can be expanded by adding channels, buckets or growing units.
